What problem the model solves, for whom, with which data, and how success is measured before any model is trained.

The approved problem

Solid waste piles up in Bogotá's public space before anyone records it. National figures describe what reaches landfills (12.1 million tonnes in Colombia in 2024, SSPD, 2025), not where waste accumulates on the street, so collection reacts to critical points once they exist. LocWaste collects citizen reports and anticipates, for each of the 112 urban UPZ (urban planning zones), whether next month's rate of accumulation reports will be low, medium or high.

Stakeholders

  • UAESP, the city unit that plans waste collection and registers critical points.
  • Collection operators, who schedule crews and routes.
  • Local mayor's offices, responsible for public space in each locality.
  • Citizens, who report accumulation points and live with their effects.

Intended beneficiaries

  • Residents of zones with recurrent accumulation (less waste, fewer rodents and odors).
  • Collection planners, who get a ranked list of zones instead of reacting to complaints.
  • Awareness campaigns, which can target the zones at risk before the peak.

From business objectives to Machine Learning objectives

Business objectiveMachine Learning objective
Send collection to the zones that will need it firstClassify each UPZ's next-month report rate as low, medium or high
Do not miss the worst zonesMaximize recall of the high class; keep high zones predicted as low near zero
Do not flag the whole cityBalanced performance across classes: macro-F1 as the main metric
Keep working as the city changesRetrain from the app and monitor drift and live performance

Questions the model answers

  1. Which UPZ will have a high rate of accumulation reports next month?
  2. How confident is that prediction (class probabilities)?
  3. Which factors drive the risk of a zone (feature importance)?

Learning task: supervised multiclass classification on a panel of zone-months (tabular data ordered in time). The unit of analysis is one UPZ in one calendar month; the target is the level of the following month.

Data quality, relevance and sufficiency

  • Quality: no missing values in the fields the model uses, {{ q.reports.exact_duplicates }} exact duplicates, {{ q.reports.invalid_categories.values()|sum }} invalid categories and {{ q.reports.updated_before_reported }} reports updated before being reported. Only the neighborhood name is missing in {{ q.reports.missing.neighborhood }} reports (points outside a cadastral sector); it is not a model input. Details in phase 2.
  • Relevance: the features describe recent history, report details and the territory, which is what collection planners use; critical points and density are known drivers of dumping.
  • Sufficiency: no public dataset reports accumulation by zone and month, so the first 24 months are synthetic, calibrated on the real open data and labelled source = synthetic. Real reports are added as citizens use the app. This is the main limitation of the project.

Feasibility, limitations and risks

Technical

  • Small tabular data: a random forest trains in seconds on a free Render instance.
  • Open data is public and refreshed by the city.
  • The trained model is stored in PostgreSQL, so it survives redeploys.

Organizational

  • Value depends on citizens reporting and on administrators moderating reports.
  • Predictions support, not replace, the decisions of the collection operator.
RiskMitigation
Synthetic history differs from realityLabelled source, documented assumptions, retraining with real reports, drift checks
Uneven participation across zonesRates per inhabitant; critical points and density as features
Leakage from the futureFeatures up to month t only, cut points fitted on training months, temporal validation
False or spam reportsAdmin moderation; rejected reports never reach the model

Measurable success criteria

CriterionThresholdWhy
Macro-F1 (temporal CV and test)≥ 2 × the most-frequent baselineProves the model learns beyond the class distribution
Recall of the high class (test)≥ 0.60Most zones that will be critical are flagged
High zones predicted as low (test)≤ 10 %The costliest error is kept small
Response time of a risk check< 10 s on the free hosting tierUsable in operations

The best possible macro-F1 for this target is about 0.63, even for a model that knew the exact parameters of the data generator, because each month adds random variation that no feature can predict (see phase 4). An absolute threshold such as 0.8 is therefore not attainable for this target with this data.
