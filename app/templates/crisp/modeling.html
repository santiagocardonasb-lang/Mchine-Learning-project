Algorithms and why they fit the problem

AlgorithmWhy it was selected
Most-frequent baselineSets the floor: any useful model has to beat predicting the majority class.
Logistic regressionSimple, linear and interpretable; tests whether a linear relation between recent history and risk is enough.
Random forestCaptures non-linear effects and interactions (a spike matters more in a zone with critical points), is robust to outliers and unscaled features, and gives feature importances (Breiman, 2001).

Gradient boosting was also tried as an exploratory experiment with the same features and folds; it reached a lower CV macro-F1 (0.550) than the random forest, so it was not pursued further.

How domain knowledge shaped the model

  • Persistence: accumulation points recur, so lags, the 3-month average and the zone's historical rate were built.
  • Critical points and density: UAESP's registered points and crowded localities concentrate dumping, so both are inputs.
  • Rainy season: April–May and October–November raise accumulation, so the month of the year is an input.
  • Rates, not counts: zones differ in population, so the target and the main features are per 10,000 inhabitants.
  • The costliest error: missing a critical zone hurts more than an extra visit, so classes are weighted (balanced) and recall of the high class breaks ties.
