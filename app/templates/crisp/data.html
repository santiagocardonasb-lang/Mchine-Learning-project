Selected sources and features

Every source comes from Datos Abiertos Bogotá or from the app itself. Features were chosen because a collection planner would look at them: how many reports a zone had recently and how that compares with its usual level, what kind of accumulation it is, and the zone's fixed traits.

Transformations

  1. Zone detection: each report's coordinates are placed in its locality, UPZ and neighborhood (point in polygon).
  2. Aggregation: reports are grouped by UPZ and calendar month; months without reports get zeros.
  3. Rates: counts are divided by the UPZ population (locality population split by area) × 10,000, so zones of different size are comparable.
  4. Lags and windows: lags 1–3, a 3-month average, the change vs last month and the historical rate, all computed with data up to month t.
  5. Shares: boolean report attributes become shares of the month's reports (0–1), so no categorical encoding is needed.
  6. Scaling: standardization only for logistic regression, inside a pipeline fitted on the training folds; trees need no scaling.
  7. Target: next month's rate is split into terciles (low, medium, high) with cut points computed on the training months only: {{ pt.cuts_from_training_months|join(" and ") }}.

Training, validation and test partitions

The data is ordered in time, so partitions are made by whole months, never at random.

  • Training: {{ pt.train|length }} months, {{ pt.train[0] }} to {{ pt.train[-1] }} ({{ "{:,}".format(pt.train_rows) }} rows).
  • Validation: five-fold TimeSeriesSplit inside the training months; every fold trains on earlier months than it validates on.
  • Test: the last {{ pt.test|length }} months, {{ pt.test[0] }} to {{ pt.test[-1] }} ({{ "{:,}".format(pt.test_rows) }} rows), used once to report final performance.
